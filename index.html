What this whole field
is actually about

A plain map of the territory, before any server or acronym. Everything in the next 21 days is one region of this picture.

The whole field hangs on one sentence:

◆ THE CORE IDEA

IAM answers two questions about every attempt to use something: "who are you?" and "are you allowed?" Every protocol, product, and job title here is a more sophisticated way of answering one of those two.

Why the job exists

With five people, access is trivial. With five thousand across three countries using two hundred apps — where people join, move, and leave every day — the two questions get genuinely hard. Proving people are who they claim, deciding what each can touch, and keeping that correct as everything changes is the whole discipline. Scale is what turns a non-problem into a career.

▲ DON'T BLUR THESE

Security is the whole field of protecting systems. IAM is the part about identity and access. A firewall is security, not IAM. "Priya sees the ledger but not the source code" is IAM.

The two words underneath everything

These get confused more than any pair in the field — both start with "au" and get shortened to look alike. Learn them cold and half of what follows is easier.

Authentication (authn)
Proving who you are. Password, security key, fingerprint.
Authorization (authz)
Deciding what you may do, once identified. Open a file, approve a budget, delete an account.
◆ MENTAL MODEL

An office building. Authentication is the guard checking your ID at the door. Authorization is which floors your card actually opens. You can be definitely-you and still refused the executive floor.

They matter because they fail differently. When you later meet invalid credentials versus insufficient rights, you'll already know which question failed — and that's where you look.

Identity: the thing being checked

An identity is the record a system keeps about someone. For a person it's an account — a username, a way to prove it's them, and attributes like department and manager. The catch worth absorbing now: not every identity is a person.

Three ways to decide "are you allowed?"

Authorization has grown in three layers. You'll hear all three constantly — meet them as a progression, not three unrelated terms.

Why the field has two of everything

On-premises means the company owns the servers, often in its own building — where identity lived for decades. Cloud means renting computing from Microsoft, Amazon, or Google over the internet. Apps moved to the cloud, and identity had to follow. Microsoft's cloud identity service, Entra ID (formerly Azure AD), is the cloud counterpart to on-prem Active Directory.

◆ MENTAL MODEL

On-prem is owning a house — full control, full responsibility. Cloud is renting an apartment — someone else fixes the plumbing, but you follow their rules and share the building.

The shape of the 21 days

You don't need to memorise this — just know the map exists. Each day zooms into one region, always circling back to one of the two questions in a harder setting.
